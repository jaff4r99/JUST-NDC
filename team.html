Framework & Methodology Instruction Manual

Welcome to the UNU-EHS FAST team's NDC evaluation hub. If you are new to this project (e.g., incoming researchers or student assistants), this page outlines exactly how we conduct our line-by-line analysis of NDC 3.0 documents to evaluate their commitments to transformative climate justice.

1. The Transformative Climate Justice Framework

Recognitional Justice

Acknowledges marginalized groups, Indigenous rights, diverse values and knowledge systems, addressing exclusion and power imbalances.

Procedural Justice

Ensures transparent, inclusive and fair decision-making processes with meaningful participation for all affected groups.

Distributive Justice

Equitably shares the benefits and burdens of climate actions and impacts across groups, scales and generations.

Restorative Justice

Recognizes and seeks to address past climate injustices or historical responsibilities through reparative measures and responses.

Transformative Climate Justice

Addressing the root causes of climate change and social injustice.

2. The Line-by-Line Coding Guide

To process our corpus of 120 NDC documents at scale while maintaining rigorous qualitative standards, we follow a strict hybrid workflow. Here is what you need to do step-by-step:

  1. Review the Extracted Text: Instead of reading hundreds of pages manually, an AI (NLP) tool will scrape the PDFs and pre-flag paragraphs containing implicit or explicit mentions of justice. Your job begins with reviewing these flagged text excerpts.
  2. Interpret & Categorize the Dimension: Read the specific statement carefully. Use the four definitions listed in the framework diagram above to decide which dimension of climate justice (Recognitional, Procedural, Distributive, or Restorative) the text aligns with.
  3. Assess the Level of Commitment: Next, you must grade how strong the policy commitment actually is. We use a four-tier grading scale. Evaluate the excerpt against the Matrix Table below to see if it qualifies as No Mention, Tokenistic, Partial, or Embedded. Pay special attention to whether the statement is just a vague aspiration (Tokenistic) or backed by a legally binding institutional framework (Embedded).

3. Commitment Grading Matrix

Justice dimension No mention (absent) Tokenistic justice (weak commitment) Partial justice (moderate commitment) Embedded justice (strong commitment)

Procedural justiceNo mention of participatory governance, transparency or stakeholder involvement.Mentions participation, transparency or stakeholder engagement but without specifying solid mechanisms for implementation.Specifies participatory governance, transparency or stakeholder engagement mechanisms, but these are not backed by formal institutional structures or legal frameworks.Participatory governance, transparency and stakeholder engagement mechanisms are backed by formal institutional structures or legal frameworks, with guaranteed implementation and accountability.
Distributive justiceNo mention of equitable allocation of resources, finance or benefits.Broadly references equity or allocation of resources or finance but without specifying any mechanisms to ensure delivery.Specifies allocation mechanisms for resources, finance or benefits, but these are not backed by formal institutional structures or legal frameworks.Equitable allocation of resources, finance or benefits is backed by formal institutional structures or legal frameworks, with monitoring and accountability mechanisms.
