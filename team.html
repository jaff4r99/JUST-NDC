Framework and Methodology

This page is the working manual for the JUST-NDC analysis. It sets out why the project exists, the framework we apply, how a statement is coded, what the tool does on its own, and where its judgement stops and yours begins. If you are joining the team, read it end to end before you code anything.

Sections 4 to 11 set out the scoring method from end to end: how a sentence becomes a tier, how tiers become a dimension score, and how four dimension scores become a band.

1. Why this project exists

why the project exists and what it is testing

Climate change is a lived experience that magnifies existing inequalities within and between countries. Nationally Determined Contributions are the core vehicle through which states set out what they will do about it. Under the Paris Agreement each Party is expected to prepare, communicate and maintain successive NDCs, to do so in the light of equity, and to make each one represent a progression beyond the last and reflect its highest possible ambition.

The expectation is not only that countries cut emissions but that they explain how their plans are fair. The technical dialogue of the first global stocktake states that all countries are expected to explain in their NDCs how those NDCs are fair and ambitious. The UAE Consensus agreed at COP28 went further, urging governments to embed inclusive, multilevel and gender-responsive governance in their new plans. Civil society has framed the test more bluntly: whether governments move beyond tokenistic mentions of inclusive language towards operationalising measures that make climate action equitable.

NDC 3.0, the round due in 2025 and covering targets to 2035, is the first cycle in which that expectation can be tested at scale. That is what this project does. It asks whether NDCs simply acknowledge justice or actively work on inequities, by rating statements and paying attention to the legal, institutional and financial backing behind them.

Framework source: Abbas, S. M. J., Sandholz, S. and van Schie, D. (2025). Tokenistic or transformative? Evaluating transformative climate justice in NDC 3.0 submissions. UNU-EHS Policy Brief No. 16. The framework builds on IPCC (2022) and EEA (2024), the transformative justice agenda set out by Newell and others (2021), and earlier justice coding work by Juhola and others (2022) and Jones and others (2024).

2. The four dimensions of climate justice

recognitional, procedural, distributive, restorative

Recognitional Justice

Acknowledges marginalized groups, Indigenous rights, diverse values and knowledge systems, addressing exclusion and power imbalances.

Procedural Justice

Ensures transparent, inclusive and fair decision-making processes with meaningful participation for all affected groups.

Distributive Justice

Equitably shares the benefits and burdens of climate actions and impacts across groups, scales and generations.

Restorative Justice

Recognizes and seeks to address past climate injustices or historical responsibilities through reparative measures and responses.

Transformative Climate Justice

Addressing the root causes of climate change and social injustice.

Transformative climate justice is not a fifth dimension and is never coded on a statement. The four dimensions together establish a baseline. Transformative justice is reached at their intersection, when a country shifts power, rules and resource flows rather than adding stand-alone measures. Every statement in this project is coded to one of the four. The transformative judgement is made once, at country level, after all four have been assessed. Section 6 sets out exactly how.

3. The line-by-line coding guide

the five steps a coder follows

We process 121 NDC documents while keeping qualitative standards. The workflow is hybrid: the tool retrieves and proposes, you decide.

  1. Review the retrieved text. The screener reads the PDF and pre-flags sentences containing explicit or implicit justice content. Your work starts with those, plus the passages the recall net caught that the classifier could not assign.
  2. Assign the dimension. Decide which of the four dimensions the statement belongs to, using the definitions above. The tool proposes one and shows which terms drove it, along with any close alternative. Where two dimensions are close, it says so rather than picking silently.
  3. Grade the level of commitment. Use the matrix in section 4. The question is not whether the measure sounds good but how it is phrased: a vague aspiration is tokenistic, a named measure without backing is partial, and a measure tied to law, institutions, funding and accountability is embedded.
  4. Correct the machine, do not trust it. Every entry shows the evidence behind its grade. Where it is wrong, reassign it in the ledger. Your correction is what enters the dataset. Treat the machine label as a first draft written by a fast but literal-minded assistant.
  5. Record the reason. Every override takes a short note. Two coders disagreeing is normal and useful. Two coders disagreeing silently is not.

4. The four steps: from one sentence to one verdict

sentence, dimension, axis, country

The assessment runs in four steps. Each step decides one thing and passes its result to the next. No step skips ahead. A single sentence never produces a country verdict, and a country verdict is never read back onto a single sentence.

Step 1 · the sentence

Dimension and tier

Each coded sentence is placed in one of the four dimensions and given one of three commitment tiers, on the evidence that sentence contains.

out: Procedural · Partial
Step 2 · the dimension

A score out of 100

The statements in a dimension are added together with diminishing returns. The total is then converted back into a tier.

out: Procedural = 52 / 100, Partial
Step 3 · the axes

Two axes, one grid

The four dimensions are paired into two axes. Each axis takes the lower of its two dimensions. The pair fixes the country's position on the 3×3 matrix.

out: R+P Partial, D+R Tokenistic
Step 4 · the country

Score, checks, band

A country score combines the average and the lowest of the four dimension scores. Six checks then decide whether the band that score reaches is confirmed or held back.

out: band 3, Partial commitment
Why every step takes the lower reading. Transformative climate justice is defined here as the intersection of the four dimensions rather than their sum. Each step therefore takes the lower of the readings available to it. An axis takes the weaker of its two dimensions. Half of the country score is the lowest dimension score. The checks ask what the weakest dimension is before they ask what the strongest is. A country with one strong dimension and three weak ones scores low.

5. Step 1: grading a single statement

tokenistic, partial, embedded, with examples

Each coded sentence receives one of three tiers. A fourth label, absent, applies to a dimension the document never mentions.

The tier describes how a claim is written and what it is attached to. It does not describe whether the policy behind it is a good one. A broad promise with nothing behind it is tokenistic. A modest measure written into law with a budget line is embedded.

TOKENISTIC
The Government recognises the importance of stakeholder participation in climate planning.
Why: a mention with nothing attached. No measure, no responsible body, no date. Nothing in the sentence could be checked in three years' time.
PARTIAL
A national climate consultation forum will convene civil society, indigenous organisations and youth representatives twice a year to review implementation of this NDC.
Why: a commitment verb, a named measure, a named body and a frequency. What is missing is the anchoring. No law requires it, no budget funds it, and nothing follows if it does not happen.
EMBEDDED
Under Article 12 of the Climate Framework Act (No. 70/2019), the National Climate Council shall publish every sectoral plan for a 60-day public comment period, and the Ministry of Finance allocates USD 2 million a year to support participation by affected communities; compliance is reported annually to Parliament.
Why: a named law, a named institution, a budget line, a deadline and a reporting duty. A commitment written this way survives a change of minister.

These three sentences were written for this page to show the ladder clearly. They are not quotations from any NDC.

The eight kinds of evidence. The classifier does not look for a single word. It tests each sentence for eight kinds of evidence, and the tier follows from which of them are present:

legal anchor: a named act, decree or article institution anchor: a named body made responsible finance anchor: a budget line or monetary figure time bound: a date, deadline or frequency accountability: monitoring, reporting or enforcement commitment verb: will, shall, commits, undertakes concrete measure: a named programme or instrument cited instrument: an external plan or convention invoked

Three further signals adjust the reading rather than adding to it: hedging (may, aims to, where feasible), conditionality (subject to international support), and negation, where a law is named only to say that it does not apply.

The rule in short. Embedded: a named legal instrument, or a named institution together with money, plus a commitment verb, plus either an accountability provision or a deadline, with the anchor not negated. Partial: a commitment verb plus a named basis, meaning a measure, an institution, a deadline or a cited external instrument, without legal or financial anchoring. Hedged language stays at partial. Tokenistic: everything else, including a duty stated with no measure attached, and any sentence whose anchor is negated.

6. Step 2: scoring a dimension

the formula, worked through

Why a dimension is scored rather than read off its strongest sentence. A dimension could be graded by taking the highest tier that any one of its sentences reached. That method is simple to explain, and imprecise in practice. One anchored sentence among twenty mentions would grade the whole dimension embedded, and the grade would rest on whichever sentence happened to be strongest. Scoring the dimension instead gives a reading that reflects how much of the document stands behind the grade. Several commitments count for more than one. Repetition counts for nothing.

Why the score is not a percentage. A share of statements is not used anywhere in this project. Dividing by the number of statements makes the result depend on the length of the document. An NDC with 80 mentions and 3 anchored commitments would score 4 per cent, while a twelve-page NDC with 3 mentions and 1 anchored commitment would score 33 on weaker evidence. Length is a drafting choice. What the score accumulates is evidence, and it accumulates it with diminishing returns.

for each statement i in the dimension:
  v = T(tier) + B × (features present / 8)   ← evidence value
  v = v × (every penalty that applies)   ← discounted value

order the statements by v, strongest first, then:
  S = v₁ + 0.6·v₂ + 0.36·v₃ + 0.216·v₄ …
  score = 100 × S / 4.0   (capped at 100)

What each term does.

  • T(tier) is 0 for tokenistic, 1.0 for partial and 2.0 for embedded. A tokenistic statement is a mention without an actionable commitment, so it adds nothing to a measure of commitment. Adding more tokenistic sentences cannot raise the score, and it cannot lower it either. Letting them dilute the score would be a percentage by another name, and would count length against a document that says more.
  • B = 0.5 is the evidence bonus. A statement carrying all eight kinds of evidence adds the full 0.5. One carrying two adds 0.125. An embedded commitment that names a law, a budget, a deadline and a monitoring duty therefore counts for more than one that names a law alone.
  • 0.6 is the decay. The strongest statement counts in full, the second at 0.6, the third at 0.36. Any number of identical statements converges on 2.5 times the value of one, so volume cannot inflate a dimension. Two independent commitments are still worth clearly more than one.
  • 4.0 is the reference. Two statements graded embedded, each carrying all eight kinds of evidence, add up to 2.5 + (0.6 × 2.5) = 4.0 and score 100. Every other document is read against that.
  • The penalties multiply rather than subtract. Conditional on external support ×0.80. Sitting in a vision or context section rather than an operational one ×0.80. Dimension flagged for manual confirmation ×0.85. Tier resting on evidence outside the coded sentence ×0.90. A negated anchor ×0, which removes the statement from the total.

Turning the score back into a tier. Below 35 the dimension reads tokenistic. From 35 to 69 it reads partial. From 70 upwards it reads embedded. Within 5 points of either threshold the dimension is marked borderline, because one coding change would move it.

Two results that follow from the formula.
1. No single sentence can make a dimension embedded. The highest value one statement can reach is 2.5, meaning embedded, all eight kinds of evidence, no penalties. That scores 62.5, below the threshold of 70. An embedded dimension always rests on at least two separate commitments.
2. One partial statement on its own does not make a dimension partial. A partial sentence with no evidence features scores 25. Two of them score 40. A dimension reaches partial when the document returns to the subject with something new.

The score can lower a grade but never raise it. A dimension is never graded above the strongest statement it actually contains. Twenty-five named measures with no legal, institutional or financial backing are still not an institutionalised commitment, and no accumulation of them is allowed to read as one. The score can therefore confirm or lower the grade that the strongest statement would give, and cannot raise it. Where this cap applies, the dimension card says so.

Confidence is derived, not assigned. Each dimension reports high, medium or low confidence. It begins at high and drops one level for each of the following: the score rests on a single statement; the score sits within 5 points of a threshold; every statement carrying the grade is flagged for manual confirmation of its dimension; every one of them is conditional on external support; every one of them sits in a framing section rather than an operational one. The dimension card lists whichever of these applied. A tokenistic dimension is reported at high confidence, because a finding that nothing in it is actionable leaves little room for doubt.

7. Step 3: the two axes and the matrix

pairing the four dimensions into two axes

The four dimensions are paired into two axes:

  • Recognitional and procedural, the vertical axis: whether the NDC identifies affected groups and gives them a way into decisions.
  • Distributive and restorative, the horizontal axis: whether resources actually move to those groups, and whether past harm is addressed.

Each axis takes the lower of its two dimensions. A country with embedded recognitional justice and tokenistic procedural justice sits at tokenistic on the vertical axis. The pairing treats the two dimensions as a set that has to hold together, rather than averaging them. The rule is a named setting in the code (AXIS_RULE), so it can be changed and reported rather than changed quietly.

It is not printed as a label on the matrix, because a label over a cell would apply to every country in that cell on the basis of position alone, and because the pattern occurs at more than one level of commitment.

8. Step 4: the country score and the six checks

half the mean, half the floor, and six checks

Two things happen at country level. An arithmetic score proposes a band, and six qualitative checks decide whether the country keeps it.

country score = 0.5 × average of the four dimension scores
               + 0.5 × lowest of the four

Half of the country score is the weakest dimension. A country with two dimensions at 80 and two at 0 has an average of 40 and a lowest score of 0, giving a country score of 20. A country with all four dimensions at 20 also scores 20. The framework treats those two documents as equally far from transformative justice.

The six checks. Each returns pass, partial or fail, and each is reported with its evidence on the country record.

1
Coverage
Are all four justice dimensions addressed at all?
Pass on four present, partial on three, fail below that. This is the only check that asks about breadth.
2
Depth
What is the weakest of the four dimensions?
Pass only when all four are embedded, partial when all four are at least partial. This check decides whether the top band is reachable.
3
Balance
Is the recognitional and procedural axis matched by the distributive and restorative axis?
Pass when both axes are embedded, partial when both are at least partial. A justice-washing pattern fails here.
4
Anchoring
Are justice commitments tied to named law, institutions or money?
Pass needs a legal anchor in at least one dimension and an institutional or financial anchor in at least one. Either on its own returns partial.
5
Integration
Does justice appear in the operational parts of the NDC, or only in the framing?
Pass needs three of the four dimensions carrying actionable statements outside the vision and context sections, and presence in at least four of the nine thematic clusters. Justice confined to a preamble fails here.
6
Structural engagement
Does the NDC address the causes of injustice or only its symptoms?
Pass on at least one structural statement at partial or above, or on the NDC defining climate justice and linking that definition to policy alongside two structural statements.
How the score and the checks combine. The score sets the band. The top band carries one extra condition: a score of 70 or more is not sufficient on its own, because at least five of the six checks must also pass and the depth check must not fail. Where the score reaches the top band and the checks do not, the country is held one band lower and the record names the checks that failed. The checks can hold a country back. They cannot move one up.

Three flags travel alongside the band rather than inside it: process-heavy and outcome-light, band not stable (a dimension sits within 5 points of a threshold, so small coding changes could move the country), and low confidence in at least one dimension. A flag is a prompt to read the record.

9. The six bands

the ladder from absent to approaching transformative

Six bands describe a document as a whole. Each band describes what the document does. None of them describes the country behind it.

0absent

Justice largely absent

No statement in the document was assigned to any of the four dimensions. The NDC is a technical document that does not engage justice.

Reached when no dimension has a single coded statement. The extraction diagnostics should be checked before this is recorded, since a scanned PDF produces the same result for a different reason.
1tokenistic

Tokenistic

Justice appears as language. Groups are named, participation is affirmed and equity is invoked, and no dimension carries an actionable commitment behind any of it.

Country score below 15. In practice, a document whose justice content is entirely mentions.
2emergent

Emergent commitment

Isolated measures have been attached to the justice language, but they do not yet form a pattern and nothing is anchored. One dimension may be well developed while the others are language only. Since half of the country score is the lowest dimension, that unevenness holds the band here.

Country score 15 to 34.
3partial

Partial commitment

Named measures across more than one dimension, without legal, institutional or financial anchoring. This is the most common band in the corpus. These documents contain real measures, none of which is yet written into anything that outlives the drafting.

Country score 35 to 54.
4substantive

Substantive but uneven

Anchored commitments are present, but the four dimensions do not hold together and the lowest sits well below the highest. This band also takes the country whose score reached band 5 while the checks did not, in which case the record names the checks that failed.

Country score 55 to 69, or 70 and above with fewer than five checks passing.
5approaching

Approaching transformative climate justice

All four dimensions carry anchored commitments, the two axes are balanced, and the NDC addresses the causes of injustice as well as its symptoms. The word approaching is deliberate: the evidence here is a document, and arrival is a matter of implementation.

Country score 70 or above, at least five of the six checks passing, and the depth check not failing.
Tier or band? The two scales are easy to confuse in writing. A tier (absent, tokenistic, partial, embedded) describes a statement or a dimension. A band (0 to 5, the ladder above) describes a country. A country in band 3 may contain several embedded statements, and a dimension graded embedded does not place its country in band 5.

11. Frequently asked questions

the questions that come up most

Why is transformative climate justice not a fifth dimension?

Because it describes how the four dimensions fit together, rather than a kind of content that a sentence can contain. It is never coded on a statement. It is judged once, at country level, after all four dimensions have been assessed. Treated as a fifth dimension, it could be scored well by a country that failed the other four, which is the reading the framework is built to prevent.

Why does the number of justice mentions not affect the result?

Because it measures how much a country wrote, rather than what it committed to. Tokenistic statements are worth zero, so repeating them changes nothing, and the decay means the fourth anchored commitment adds about a fifth of what the first added. A 460-page NDC and a 14-page NDC are assessed on the same terms. Counts are still reported, under a heading stating that they feed no score, because they describe the corpus and are useful in a paper.

Is taking the lower dimension on each axis unfair to a country that does one thing well?

It is a strict rule, and it follows from the definition of transformative justice used here rather than from convenience. Recognition without redistribution is the pattern the policy brief documents. A country that consults widely and moves no resources has completed the cheaper half of the work. Both readings are calculated in every assessment. Where the verdict would change under the alternative rule, the record is marked rule-sensitive and the alternative verdict is named.

How can a country contain embedded statements and still be graded tokenistic?

In two ways. A single embedded statement scores 62.5, below the 70 an embedded dimension needs, so one anchored commitment leaves its dimension at partial. And half of the country score is the lowest dimension, so strength in one dimension does not compensate for silence in another. The dimension cards on the country record show which statements carried each score and what each one contributed.

What happens when the tool is wrong about a sentence?

Each statement in the ledger shows the terms that assigned its dimension, the evidence that set its tier, any close alternative dimension, and the page and line it came from. Where two dimensions are close, or where only supporting terms matched, the statement is flagged for confirmation instead of being assigned quietly. A coder's override replaces the machine label, carries a name, a timestamp and an engine version, and the country score is recalculated from the corrected set. The machine output is a first draft and the dataset is the human layer.

Can results from different engine versions be compared?

Counts cannot, and scores should not be compared directly. Retrieval changes between versions, so the number of statements in the ledger is not comparable. Compare the dimension profile and the checks instead, and state the engine and lexicon version in any published table. Both are stamped on every export and on every country record.

The method in one paragraph

Every sentence that engages climate justice is assigned to one of four dimensions and graded on how it is written: a mention, a named measure, or a measure attached to law, an institution or a budget. Each dimension is then scored out of 100 by accumulating its commitments with diminishing returns, so that neither repetition nor a single strong sentence can carry it. The four dimensions are paired into two axes, each taking the lower of its pair, and a country score is formed from half the average and half the lowest of the four dimension scores. That score proposes one of six bands, running from justice largely absent to approaching transformative climate justice. Six checks, covering coverage, depth, balance, anchoring, integration and structural engagement, can hold a country below the top band but cannot raise one. Every step is recorded against the page and line it came from, and every step can be corrected by a coder.

12. What the 2025 policy brief found

the findings the tool has to reproduce

The brief coded 27 NDCs submitted to the UNFCCC as of July 2025, line by line, by hand. Four were in Spanish and one in French; those were handled with professional translation software and secondary manual verification by fluent speakers, with discrepancies resolved through blind cross-review. Content was also organised into nine thematic clusters, guided by prevalent topics in the texts and the CAN Guidelines for NDCs, to see where justice is integrated and where it is neglected. The findings that the tool now has to reproduce at scale are these.

  • Justice language is common, embedded commitments are not. Most references remain tokenistic or partial. Many statements are high-level or process-oriented rather than specifying measures with institutional support.
  • Recognitional and procedural justice dominate. More than half of the NDCs analysed mentioned vulnerable groups or the importance of stakeholder consultation, making these the most frequently mentioned dimensions. Most of those mentions are tokenistic. Countries list vulnerable populations or affirm support for them, but only a minority outline specific programmes or legal protections. Few provide mechanisms for ongoing inclusive decision-making.
  • Distributive and restorative justice lag. Both are less prevalent. Restorative justice was the least addressed dimension, with most countries avoiding language that could be read as accepting liability or formally compensating for climate harm.
  • Loss and damage usually points outward. Where it appears, it is often an appeal for global support rather than a commitment by the country to compensate affected groups within its own territory. This is why the tool now records the direction of every claim.
  • International distributive framing tracks economic capacity. References to providing finance or technology transfer, or to common but differentiated responsibilities, were more frequent in submissions from parties with comparatively greater economic capacity.
  • The imbalance is the headline. Heavy on recognitional and procedural mentions, light on distributive and restorative action. Most countries are more comfortable acknowledging inequities than committing to redistributive or compensatory measures. This is the pattern the brief names as a justice-washing risk, and the tool flags it explicitly.
  • Justice is siloed by theme. Commitments are deepest in the just transition and social equity cluster and the capacity-building and public participation cluster, and weakest in technically focused areas such as sectoral mitigation and technology and innovation. Strong procedural commitments at national planning level rarely translate into guaranteed stakeholder influence over financial allocations within sectors.
  • A small set of countries are close. Only a few reach embedded level on both axes of the matrix. Brazil is the worked example in the brief: it defines climate justice on the first page of its NDC, links that definition to the Pact for Ecological Transformation and the National Policy on Climate Change, reports participatory design involving more than 4,000 participants and 1,200 proposals, prepares sectoral plans for indigenous peoples and for racial equality, and applies common but differentiated responsibilities within the country as well as between countries.

The brief's three recommendations shape what the tool looks for. Pair commitments with the law or policy they attach to, the responsible institution, a budget line, a timeline and a monitoring indicator. Translate participation commitments into outcomes on distribution and remediation. Adapt the drafting patterns of the countries already close to transformative justice.

13. How the tool works

retrieval, classification, and what is recorded

What kind of AI this is. It is a natural language processing pipeline, not a large language model. Nothing is sent to an external model and nothing is generated. Every decision is made by rules you can read in the page source, so the same document produces the same result every time, and any grade can be traced to the exact words that produced it. That is a deliberate choice: a black box cannot be defended in a methods section, and a generated summary cannot be checked against the text it came from. The trade-off is that the tool has no understanding. It reads patterns. That is why every output is a proposal for a human coder rather than a result.

Stage one: retrieval, tuned for recall. The document is read page by page. Lines are rebuilt from the position of each text fragment on the page rather than concatenated in file order, so two-column layouts and tables do not come out interleaved. Tables of contents, acronym lists, reference pages and cover pages are detected and skipped. Headings are tracked so every sentence knows which section of the NDC it came from. Sentences are split in a way that survives decimals, abbreviations and bullet lists, which is most of what an NDC is made of. If the text layer is thin, the document is flagged as probably scanned rather than reported as containing no justice content.

The lexicon. Terms are held in three groups. Core terms are specific enough to assign a dimension on their own. Supporting terms add weight but cannot assign a dimension by themselves. The recall net holds terms that appear near justice content but are not evidence of it, such as capacity building, climate finance, disaster risk reduction and resilience. Recall net terms flag a passage for you to look at and can never assign a dimension or a grade. This is how the tool stays wide without letting adaptation vocabulary masquerade as restorative justice. The lexicon covers English, Spanish, French, Russian, Arabic, Chinese and Ukrainian, and it carries a version number that is stamped on every export.

Explicit and implicit together. A lot of justice content carries no justice vocabulary at all. A tariff design that protects poor households, a sixty-day comment period, a right of appeal to an independent panel, a rebuilding grant after a flood: all are justice statements that a keyword screen loses completely. Alongside the lexicon the tool therefore runs a layer of phrase patterns, one set per dimension, that look for the shape of the claim rather than its vocabulary. Revenue being returned to households, targeting by poverty rather than by sector, the differentiated needs of a named group, a plan prepared jointly with those affected, losses that adaptation could not prevent. These patterns sit between core and supporting terms in weight, and anything matched on phrasing alone is tagged in the ledger as an implicit match so you can check the reading. On a test set of twenty-one implicit statements written without justice vocabulary, the lexicon alone surfaced three; with the pattern layer it surfaces twenty-one, with no increase in false positives on a control set of technical sentences.

Context is shown, the sentence is coded. Each entry displays the sentence before and after the one being graded, with the graded sentence highlighted. The coding unit stays the single sentence, so nothing is counted twice, but you never have to judge a claim stripped of what surrounds it. This matters for a specific failure mode: the earlier version flagged a sentence whenever any sentence in a three-sentence window matched, so purely technical sentences entered the ledger because a neighbour happened to mention women or indigenous peoples. Those inflated the totals without adding evidence.

Non-English documents. Two aids are provided. The term gloss runs offline and always works: it shows the English equivalent of the justice terminology the classifier actually matched, so you can see why something was flagged even in a script you do not read. Machine translation of the full sentence is available on request and calls a public translation service, so it needs a connection and is rate limited. It is for orientation only. Coding decisions on non-English documents follow the method used in the policy brief: professional translation, secondary verification by a fluent speaker, and blind cross-review of any discrepancy.

The retrieval funnel is reported, not hidden. Every run states how many sentence candidates were seen, how many were discarded as fragments or as table rows, how many were carried forward, how many were assigned a dimension and how many were caught by the recall net alone. If the numbers look wrong you can see where they went rather than having to trust a single total.

Stage two: classification, tuned for precision. Each sentence is scored across the four dimensions and assigned to the highest scoring one. There is no default dimension and no fallback. Where two dimensions are close, or where only supporting terms matched, the statement is marked for manual confirmation and both candidates are shown.

What else is recorded. Each statement also carries the direction of the claim, meaning whether it is a domestic commitment about the state's own conduct or an international claim about what others should do; whether it is conditional on external support; which section of the NDC it sits in and whether that section is framing or operational; which of the nine thematic clusters it belongs to; and whether it engages structural drivers of injustice. None of these change the dimension or the tier, but several of them discount the score, and all of them are what make the dataset worth analysing afterwards.

What the tool never does. It never applies a transformative label to a statement. It never counts mentions to reach a conclusion. It never decides that a country is doing well because it repeats a phrase.

14. What the tool cannot do

the limits, stated plainly
  • It reads what is written, not what will happen. A well-drafted commitment scores the same whether or not it is ever delivered. This limitation is stated in the policy brief and has not changed.
  • Recall is not yet established. The pipeline can only be checked on what it retrieved. Reading a stratified sample of pages the screener did not flag, roughly ten pages each from twelve documents across the language groups, is what turns the recall assumption into a number we can publish.
  • The embedded grade is strongest in English, Spanish and French. Legal and institutional vocabulary in Russian, Arabic and Chinese is thinner in the lexicon, so submissions in those languages carry a higher risk of being graded down for linguistic rather than substantive reasons. Treat non-Latin-script documents as requiring full manual confirmation.
  • Anchors are recorded, not verified. The tool notes that an NDC names a law. It does not check that the law exists or says what the NDC claims. Decide before coding whether embedded requires external verification, and log the source if it does.
  • Counts are not comparable across engine versions. The number of flagged statements dropped sharply between versions because sentences are no longer flagged for the content of their neighbours. Fewer entries in the ledger means less noise, not less coverage. Never compare a total from one engine version against another; compare the dimension profile and the rubric instead.
  • The dataset is the human layer. Machine output is a first draft. Overrides carry a coder name, a timestamp and an engine version for exactly this reason.

Dimension profile

Each dimension is scored from the evidence its statements carry, then banded. The strongest statement counts in full and each further one at a decreasing weight, so a dimension cannot be carried by a single sentence and cannot be inflated by repetition. Tokenistic statements score zero, so they neither raise a dimension nor dilute it. Open a card to see the arithmetic.

Transformative justice rubric

Transformative climate justice is not a fifth dimension and is never coded on a statement. It is the judgement made once all four dimensions have been assessed, following the intersection logic in the policy brief.
